GHG, LCA, and PCF: What's the Difference?

These three terms get used almost interchangeably in sustainability conversations. They shouldn't be — each one answers a different question, and mixing them up leads to the wrong number being used for the wrong decision.

The three terms, in plain words

GHG accounting
Greenhouse Gas accounting

How much a whole organisation emits, over a year, across everything it does. Split into Scope 1 (emissions the company creates directly), Scope 2 (emissions from the electricity it buys), and Scope 3 (everything else in its value chain — suppliers, transport, product use, waste). This is the number a company reports for its climate targets.

LCA
Life Cycle Assessment

The full environmental picture of one product, from raw material to disposal — and not just carbon. A proper LCA also looks at water use, land use, toxicity, eutrophication, and several other impact categories. It's the broadest of the three: one product, many kinds of impact.

PCF
Product Carbon Footprint

The carbon slice of an LCA. Same life-cycle thinking — raw material to disposal — but narrowed to just one impact category: greenhouse gas emissions. If an LCA asks "what's this product's total environmental impact," a PCF asks only "how much CO2e does this product's life cycle emit."

The ISO standards, compared

Each of these has its own ISO standard behind it — and one more (ISO 14025) that builds on top of them for public reporting.

Standard Covers Scope
ISO 14040 LCA — principles and framework. Defines what an LCA is and its four phases. Product
ISO 14044 LCA — requirements and guidelines. The detailed how-to that 14040 sets the frame for. Always cited alongside 14040. Product
ISO 14067 Carbon footprint of products. Uses the same life-cycle method as 14040/44, narrowed to greenhouse gases only. Product
ISO 14064 GHG quantification and verification. Part 1 covers organisation-level inventories, Part 2 covers projects, Part 3 covers verification. The ISO counterpart to the (more widely used) GHG Protocol. Organisation / project
ISO 14025 Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs). A standardised, third-party-verified format for publishing LCA results — it doesn't run the assessment itself, it standardises how the results get reported. Product
A quick way to hold this: 14040/14044 is the method, 14067 is that same method pointed only at carbon, and 14025 is the reporting format you publish the result in. 14064 sits in a different lane entirely — it's not about products at all, it's how a company counts its own emissions.

Why the distinction actually matters

Using the wrong one isn't just a labelling error. A company reporting its GHG inventory (Scope 1–3) has told you nothing about which of its products is worse for water or land use — that needs an LCA. A supplier quoting a "carbon footprint" for one product hasn't told you their company-wide emissions — that's a PCF, not GHG accounting. And a PCF on its own, however accurate, is silent on everything an LCA would have caught outside of carbon. Each one answers a real question — just not the same one.
