Why these three

Read together they describe one problem. The circular material use rate shows how small a share of Europe's material input comes from recycling rather than new extraction — the rest is still virgin material.

The two packaging figures show where the gap bites hardest. Overall packaging recycling looks reasonably healthy; plastic packaging does not. That matters because the PPWR requires binding shares of post-consumer recycled plastic in packaging from 2030, and only material that is actually collected and recycled can supply them.

The distance between those two numbers is the supply bottleneck the Circular Economy Act is meant to close.

Source and method. Data retrieved live from the Eurostat Statistics API (dissemination API, JSON-stat format) each time this page is opened. Geography is the EU27 aggregate. Each figure shows the most recent year for which Eurostat reports a value, which is not the current year — packaging statistics are published roughly two years in arrears. Packaging recycling rates use the unit defined for the 2025 target and onwards. No data is stored or modified here; if Eurostat revises a figure, this page changes with it.
