Reading these together

Overall packaging recycling looks reasonably healthy. Plastic packaging does not — and that gap is the problem, because the PPWR requires binding shares of post-consumer recycled plastic from 2030, and only material actually collected and recycled can supply them. Closing that gap is what the Circular Economy Act is meant to do.

The circular material use rate puts the packaging numbers in proportion. Even a good recycling rate in one waste stream moves the whole-economy figure very little, because packaging is a small share of total material throughput. Circularity is not a packaging problem with a packaging solution.

Sources and method. Circularity and packaging figures: Eurostat dissemination API, JSON-stat format, EU27 aggregate and Germany, datasets cei_srm030 and cei_wm020, using the recycling-rate unit defined for the 2025 target and onwards. Emissions: World Bank Indicators API v2, series EN.GHG.CO2.PC.CE.AR5, most recent non-empty year per country. Atmospheric concentration: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory global CO₂ trend series. Each figure shows the latest year or date its source reports, which is not the current date — statistical series are published in arrears, so different indicators may carry different years. Nothing here is stored or adjusted; if a source revises a figure, this page changes with it.
