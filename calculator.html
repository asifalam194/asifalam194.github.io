Carbon Footprint Calculator

An estimate of annual CO2 emissions from household electricity and car travel, using German emission factors and fleet-average consumption data.

Method. Electricity: 363 g CO2/kWh (Umweltbundesamt, German electricity mix, 2024). Fuel combustion: petrol 2.30 kg CO2/l, diesel 2.65 kg CO2/l (GHG Protocol). Assumed fleet-average consumption: petrol 7.7 l/100 km, diesel 6.8 l/100 km, electric 18.5 kWh/100 km (real-world European data). Electric vehicles are charged on the German grid and are therefore not zero-emission.

Scope. Tank-to-wheel and operational electricity only. Excludes fuel production (well-to-tank), vehicle and battery manufacturing, heating, food, and goods. Indicative estimate — not a full life cycle assessment.

Product notes

Why this tool is built the way it is.

Who it's for

Someone who wants a directional answer in under a minute — not a household auditor filling in a 20-field form, and not a compliance team needing a certifiable number. Three inputs, one output. The trade-off is precision for completion.

Data & assumptions

Electricity uses the Umweltbundesamt German grid-mix factor (363 g CO2/kWh, 2024). Fuel combustion uses GHG Protocol factors against real-world European fleet-average consumption, not manufacturer test-cycle figures — those consistently understate on-road use. All factors are static and hard-coded rather than user-editable, which keeps the tool simple but means it silently goes stale as grid mix and fleet averages shift year to year.

Trade-offs

  • Tank-to-wheel and operational electricity only — no well-to-tank, manufacturing, heating, or diet. Scope was cut to keep the input form to three fields.
  • One national grid factor, not a regional or supplier-specific one — precision was sacrificed for a tool anyone in Germany can use without knowing their utility.
  • Labelled "indicative estimate," not an LCA — the risk with any simplified calculator is implied precision it can't back up, so the copy works to prevent that.

What I'd build next

  • Add heating/gas — usually the single largest household emissions source, and its absence is the biggest gap in the current scope.
  • Version the emission factors with a visible "as of" date instead of burying them in a footnote, so staleness is a visible property of the data, not a hidden one.
  • Show the result against a national average, so a number in isolation becomes a number with context.
